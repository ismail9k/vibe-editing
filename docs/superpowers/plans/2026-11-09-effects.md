# Effects (the 17-trick reel) Implementation Plan

> Executed inline (superpowers:executing-plans). Ismail said "implement what you can and add the rest to the backlog", so he skipped the design conversation; the calls below are mine.

**Goal:** Vibe Editing can do the tricks from the reference reel (instagram.com/reel/DfgiBYTIUmV): effects that move the footage and effects that cut the speaker out of the background, restyled by the same styles as everything else.

**Architecture:** The renderer draws the footage itself (it used to sit under the canvas in the preview and under the overlay in ffmpeg). Each clip gets a cutout matte, made once with Apple Vision. Components flagged `scene: true` take over drawing the footage while they're on screen, through new painter primitives: `footage()`, `subject()` and `subjectBox()`.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-vibe-editing-design.md` (v1), plus this plan. Reference frames were sampled from the reel. Spike: Vision's person segmentation has fine hair but drops the mic; subject lifting (`VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest`) keeps the mic at 14 ms/frame. Union of the two.

## Global constraints
- Brand rules still hold: one orange accent per shot, text on orange is black, the glow is the only gradient, IBM Plex only, Nino whole pixels.
- Same inputs, same pixels: the export and snapshots use frame-accurate decoded footage; the preview uses `<video>`.
- Cutouts are macOS only (Vision through a small Swift helper compiled with `swiftc` on first use). Without a matte, `subject()` falls back to the whole frame and the app says so.
- Nothing here may break v1: all existing tests stay green.

## Decisions
- `FrameMedia { frame, matte }`: what renderFrame gets for time t. Sources are `CanvasImageSource`s of any size; the painter cover-fits them into the frame (export frames arrive already W×H).
- Mattes: `projects/<p>/media/.matte/<file>.matte.mp4`, grey H.264 at 30 fps, long side 960, upright (made from ffmpeg-decoded, autorotated frames).
- Export: ffmpeg decodes footage (rgba, W×H) and mattes (grey, W/2×H/2) to pipes; main hands one frame of each to the render window per frame; the window returns the finished frame; the encoder ffmpeg takes the video from stdin plus the audio.
- Snapshots decode one frame per time the same way.
- With no footage (Studio tiles, empty projects), scene components get a stand-in: the background plus a drawn silhouette, so they still preview.

## Tasks
1. **ffmpeg args**: `buildDecodeArgs(p, dir, 'video'|'matte', hdr, mattes)` and `buildEncodeArgs(p, dir, out, hasAudio)` replace `buildFfmpegArgs`. Tests: trims, concat, tone-map, grey matte at half size, audio concat with silence, adelay/amix, `-t D`, paths stay inside the project.
2. **Renderer media**: `renderFrame(ctx, p, lib, t, media?)`; base footage pass unless a `scene` component is active; painter `footage(o)`, `subject(o)`, `subjectBox()`. Tests with a fake context: base pass draws the frame, a scene component suppresses it, the stand-in kicks in without media, subjectBox from a synthetic matte.
3. **Export + snapshots** use decoders and `headlessMedia(i)`. Verify: render `talk`, md5-stable reruns, snapshot over footage.
4. **Mattes**: `native/vibe-matte.swift`, `src/main/matte.ts` (`mattePath`, `ensureMatte` with progress), `vibe matte <project>`, the app makes them in the background when a project uses a matte component. Verify on Ismail's clip.
5. **Preview**: draws `<video>` and the matte video into the canvas; Studio tiles keep the stand-in.
6. **Effects** (components): checklist, chart-grow, digital-zoom, phone, zoom-cut, backdrop (green screen), paper-cut, doodle-boil, cover-story, frame-break, orbit, vertigo, wall (couch / headline wall), glass-type. Each verified by snapshot on real footage and on the stand-in, in two styles.
7. README, CLAUDE.md (workspace guide: the new primitives and components), backlog, commit.

## Review focus
1. Matte and footage out of step (preview drift, wrong rotation): the cutout halo must not lag visibly.
2. No matte yet / Swift missing: effects still draw, the app says why, export refuses with a clear message.
3. A project with no footage: the export still renders (the base layer is the background).
4. Memory: 8 MB frames over IPC must not pile up (one in flight).
5. HDR clips: the matte is made from tone-mapped frames, so cutouts look right.
